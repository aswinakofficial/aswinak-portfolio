# Style guide

## Voice

The post should read like someone who actually went there.

- ✅ "We reached Samse around 3 PM, and since the trek was only the next morning, we had an entire evening to kill."
- ❌ "Samse offers visitors an excellent opportunity to explore the surrounding region before beginning their trek."

Ask of every sentence: *would a real traveller actually write this?*

## Structure

A useful default arc — use it only when it serves the story:

1. **Before** — what was planned
2. **The problem** — what went wrong
3. **The adaptation** — how the plan changed
4. **The journey** — what actually happened
5. **The experience** — the trek/place itself
6. **The return** — the final challenge or surprise
7. **The lesson / reflection** — what was learned, why it mattered

Chronological works when the reader should live the trip alongside the author; topical works when practical information dominates. A hybrid — narrative first, then a compact practical section (how to reach, booking, cost, what to carry, mistakes to avoid) — is usually best. Tell the story once; give the takeaway once.

## Introduction

Open with the actual experience, not destination boilerplate. It must answer: *why read this particular story?*

- ❌ "Kudremukh is a beautiful trekking destination in Karnataka known for its lush green hills."
- ✅ "Our Kudremukh trek wasn't supposed to happen. We were planning Netravati until it was closed at the last minute. What followed was a 22 km trek, a 240 km scooter ride and one very strict 10 PM deadline."

## Pacing

Spend words on turning points, surprises, conversations, emotional or visually strong moments, useful discoveries and lessons. Compress routine transitions, meals and logistics.

- ❌ "Then we got ready. Then we went downstairs. Then we started walking."
- ✅ "By 6 AM, we were ready to leave."

## Show, don't tell

Use concrete details from the notes — never invented ones.

- ❌ "The trek was difficult." → ✅ "Rain had turned the soil into mud, and people were slipping on the steeper sections."
- ❌ "The journey was scenic." → ✅ "The road disappeared into forest sections, and the rain made the ride feel nothing like the morning commute."

## Redundancy example

- ❌ Mentioning the 10 PM scooter deadline four times in four sections.
- ✅ Establish it once as stakes: "We had one hard deadline: the scooter had to be back in Mangalore before 10 PM. After a 22 km trek, we still had roughly 120 km to ride."

## Editorial judgment

You may cut an interesting detail that doesn't contribute, merge sections that overlap, create a section for practical info that deserves one, and keep a paragraph that matters to the author's voice even if it does nothing for SEO. You are an editor, not a transcription service.
